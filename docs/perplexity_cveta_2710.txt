For this brief, the strongest starting points are “Copper + Graphite” for a premium equipment
supplier and “Green Patina + Brass” for a more distinctive engineering-services brand. Below
are six complete directions without navy, blue or cyan—each based on a material, engineering
process or technical visual convention.
The HEX values are original proposals, not colours extracted from the reference brands.
“Premium” and “trustworthy” describe the intended art direction, not a claim that a particular
hue automatically creates B2B trust.
Deep research: I am designing a website for a Russian
engineering company (HVAC, ventilation, climate and
engineering equipment supply, installation, service;
replacing European equipment). The main
competitor's site uses standard navy blue and light
blue with white. I need 4-6 unusual, memorable
colour palettes tied to engineering that avoid
standard blue and cyan. Think about real engineering
colour language: heat-treated steel and anodised
metals, copper and brass piping, safety-coding
colours for ducts and pipes, blueprint/cyanotype
alternatives, thermal-imaging gradients, industrial
coatings (RAL), oxidised patina. For each palette give:
a name, 4-5 hex colours with roles (background, text,
primary, accent, button), why it ﬁts engineering and
climate/ventilation, how it looks premium and
trustworthy for B2B clients (developers, installers,
commercial objects), contrast/accessibility notes
(WCAG AA for text and buttons), and 1-2 real websites
or brand examples using a similar idea. Cite sources.
WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for ordinary text, including most button labels, and 3:1 for large text—
at least 24 CSS px regular or approximately 18.67 CSS px bold. Meaningful control indicators
and graphical elements generally need 3:1 against adjacent colours; colour must not be the
only way to communicate status, selection or an error.
The approximate contrast ratios below are calculated from the proposed opaque sRGB colours
using WCAGʼs relative-luminance formula; they describe the speciﬁed pairings, not whole-site
compliance.

Keep branding separate from technical semantics. Actual safety markings and engineering
documentation should not be recoloured merely to match the website; their applicable
standards and project conventions take precedence.
Use RAL as a physical reference, not a HEX promise. RAL states that digital representations
approximate physical samples and recommends physical samples for ﬁnal production
decisions. Specify the websiteʼs sRGB tokens separately from paint, powder-coating and
signage speciﬁcations.
Underline inline links and label statuses. Pair colour with text, icons or shape—for
example, “В наличии,” “Под заказ” and “Требуется проверка совместимости”—rather
than relying on green, amber and red alone.
Test every interaction state. Hover, selected, focus and validation states must retain the
relevant contrast; a compliant default button does not establish compliance for its other
states.
Build credibility beyond colour. For this company, the palette should frame evidence:
replacement-selection criteria, actual project photographs, equipment speciﬁcations,
commissioning scope, warranty responsibilities and clearly stated service coverage. Avoid
visual or verbal suggestions of European manufacture or manufacturer authorisation
unless those claims are supportable.
